What it does

Four things, properly

You set the rules once. DoomBlock enforces them the moment something matches, without asking you again.

Block apps

Choose the apps that take your day. Open one during a block and the block screen is already there, detected the instant the app comes to the foreground.

Filter the web

Block whole domains or any keyword, in Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Brave and Opera, and inside the browsers built into other apps. Blocked pages bounce to a search engine you pick.

Make it stick

Schedules, session limits, a PIN, countdown gates before every way out, Strict Mode, and optional uninstall protection, so a weak moment costs more than one tap.

See where it went

Daily, weekly and custom ranges. Sort your apps into productive, neutral and distraction, and put the numbers on your home screen as a widget.

Short-form feeds

Keep the app.
Switch off the feed.

Most of the damage is one surface inside an app you have real reasons to keep. DoomBlock turns off that surface and leaves the rest working. Nine apps are covered, and each one switches independently.

  • Nine apps. YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels, TikTok videos, Snapchat Spotlight, LinkedIn video, X video on both Home and Explore, Reddit’s Popular, News, Latest and Games tabs, and Pinterest’s pin viewer.

  • Go further if you want. Stories, Explore and the home feed can each be blocked too. Those steer you back to an allowed screen with a small notice instead of a full block.

How it works

Three steps

01

Group what you block

Put apps, websites, keywords and feeds into a named group, and switch each group on independently. One group is free; a subscription lifts the cap.

02

Set the terms

A schedule, a session budget, or both. Add a PIN and cooldowns if you already know you will try to talk yourself out of it.

03

Leave it alone

A foreground service keeps watching, restarts itself after a reboot, and needs nothing from you until the schedule ends.

Questions

Before you install

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. DoomBlock is Android only today, and an iOS version is planned for the future.

Does it read my screen?

Some of it, and only to compare against your own block list:

  • which app is in the foreground;
  • the address shown in your browser’s address bar;
  • in apps like Instagram or TikTok, the on-screen labels that say which surface you are on — the Reels tab, or a DM;
  • the text of web pages opened inside other apps, if you turn that on.

Every check happens on your device and is discarded at once. Nothing is stored, nothing is sent. It cannot read your messages, your passwords, or what you type. The Privacy Policy sets this out in full.

Can I uninstall DoomBlock to get around a block?

By default, yes. Nothing stops you removing the app.

If that is the loophole you want closed, turn on Strict Mode. DoomBlock then registers as a device admin, which stops the app being uninstalled while a block is running, and turning Strict Mode back off takes your PIN and a wait of 24 hours.

That permission does nothing else. DoomBlock cannot lock your device, wipe it, or change any other setting.

What happens if my subscription lapses?

You keep one block group running, and your setup stays exactly where you left it. Groups, schedules, limits and lists are never deleted or altered — the ones over the free limit simply stop being enforced until you subscribe again.

Do I need an account, and does it work offline?

No. You can set up and use DoomBlock without signing in, and blocking never needs a connection — every decision is made on the device.

Signing in adds cloud backup and sync across devices. If you skip it, your setup is still backed up to an account with no email attached — see the Privacy Policy.

Offline, a subscription keeps working for a week before Google Play is re-checked.

Are there ads?

No. DoomBlock has no ads, no advertising SDK, and no advertising identifier. The subscription is the entire business model.
